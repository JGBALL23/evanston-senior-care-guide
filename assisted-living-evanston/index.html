Assisted living in Evanston, Illinois: how to compare your options

Start here

If a parent needs help with everyday tasks but not round-the-clock nursing care, assisted living may be worth exploring. You do not have to choose a community today. First compare the care offered, the full monthly cost, and what happens if needs change.

What assisted living usually helps with

Assisted living combines a place to live with personal assistance. Depending on the community and the resident's needs, services can include help with dressing, bathing, meals, housekeeping and medications. The exact services and extra charges vary.

It is different from independent living, which generally provides housing and conveniences rather than hands-on daily care. It is also different from skilled nursing and short-term rehabilitation. Compare the levels of care here.

How to find and check Evanston communities

Start with a shortlist, not a ranking. Online directories may mix assisted living, memory care, independent living and nursing homes even though those are different types of service.

  1. Verify the license. Use the Illinois Department of Public Health facility lookup to check the name and license type. The IDPH assisted-living page explains state oversight.
  2. Confirm the actual care offered. Describe the help your parent needs now. Ask what the community can provide and what would require a transfer later.
  3. Ask for a written price breakdown. Separate housing, meals and personal care from medication assistance, one-time fees and possible increases.
  4. Visit more than one place. Look at daily life, talk with staff, and compare answers on the same checklist instead of relying only on a brochure.

Licenses and availability can change. Verify both directly before making a decision.

What does assisted living cost in Evanston?

The CareScout 2025 Cost of Care Survey, published in 2026, reports an Illinois statewide median of $6,219 per month for assisted living. That is not an Evanston quote, and it does not tell you what a particular apartment or care package costs.

Get a dated, written estimate for your parent's assessed needs. Our assisted-living cost guide explains the charges that can surprise families. Medicare generally does not pay for long-term assisted-living care.

Six questions to take on every tour

  1. What daily help is included in the quoted price?
  2. How are changing care needs assessed, and what costs more?
  3. Who is on site overnight, and how does a resident ask for help?
  4. What happens after a fall, hospital stay, or change in memory?
  5. What are the move-in fees, annual increases and exit terms?
  6. Can you put the complete expected monthly price in writing?

Would another type of care fit better?

If memory loss or wandering creates safety concerns, compare memory care. If someone needs ongoing 24-hour nursing care or recovery after a hospital stay, ask about skilled nursing or rehabilitation. If your parent may need memory care in Evanston

In Illinois, the state licenses assisted living, memory care areas included. The Illinois Department of Public Health's 2026–2029 Alzheimer's plan says of its Division of Assisted Living: “This is a state licensure program with no federal oversight, as the residents in both assisted living and memory care areas are private pay through an establishment contract.” The same plan says staff serving people with dementia in an assisted living facility providing Alzheimer's or dementia services are required to take “six hours of initial dementia training and three hours of annual dementia training thereafter.” On a tour, ask whether the memory care area is part of the community's state licence, look the name up in the IDPH facility lookup, and ask how many hours of dementia training the staff who would care for your parent have completed. The plan adds that “Alzheimer's services supervisors in skilled nursing facilities and assisted living facilities are required to take 12 hours of annual dementia training”, so ask who supervises the memory care program and when that training was last done. It also says Illinois has an Alzheimer's Disease and Related Dementias Special Care Disclosure Act that “requires licensed residential care settings that provide specialized care to individuals with dementia to disclose information about their program to the state agency responsible for licensing of that setting”. Ask a community that offers memory care to show you the program information it disclosed.

If paying privately is not possible, the same plan describes Illinois' Supportive Living Program as an alternative to nursing home care for low-income older people, with Medicaid waiver funding for services and the resident paying room and board. It says supply for dementia care is short: “There are currently 20 SLP dementia care settings with approximately 520 apartments.” That figure is for the whole state. See how the Supportive Living Program works.

While you decide, the City of Evanston keeps a Dementia Friendly Evanston resources page. It lists the Evanston Virtual Memory Café, which meets “Weekly on Thursdays from 2-3pm” and needs no registration, and an in-person memory café at Robert Crown Community Center, “Monthly on 3rd Saturday from 1 pm – 2 pm”, with registration by phone at 847-448-8630 or online. It also lists adult day services, caregiver support groups and links on choosing and paying for long-term care.
