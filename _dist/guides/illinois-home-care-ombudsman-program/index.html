What to do when your parent's in-home care isn't working in Illinois

Short answer

If your parent gets home care through an Illinois Medicaid managed care plan (a D-SNP) or one of four Home and Community Based Services waivers — Persons who are Elderly, Persons with Disabilities, Persons with Brain Injury, or Persons with HIV or AIDS — the Illinois Department on Aging's Home Care Ombudsman Program is a free, confidential advocate for exactly this situation: a denial or termination letter, an unresponsive care coordinator, or a service that is not being delivered as promised. Call the Senior HelpLine at 1-800-252-8966 (711 TRS), press 4 for “other services,” and ask for a Home Care Ombudsman.

An ombudsman for people who never left home

Most families who search for an Illinois “ombudsman” find the Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program, which advocates for residents of nursing homes and other care facilities — covered in How to report a problem at an Illinois care facility. Fewer families know there is a separate, parallel program for people who get paid care at home instead: the Illinois Department on Aging's Home Care Ombudsman Program. The department's page on it states that the Illinois Act on Aging was amended in 2013 to authorize “advocacy services outside of the long-term care setting,” and that the program began providing that advocacy and assistance in 2014.

Who it actually serves

This program is not for every family paying for help at home — its page describes two specific routes in. The first is anyone enrolled in a Dual Eligible Special Needs Plan (D-SNP) and receiving services through a managed care organisation. The second is anyone receiving services through one of four Illinois Home and Community Based Services (HCBS) Waiver Programs: Persons who are Elderly, Persons with Disabilities, Persons with Brain Injury, or Persons with HIV or AIDS. If your parent's home care is arranged through one of those routes, this program is built for exactly their situation.

What it can actually do for you

The department describes home care ombudsmen as people who answer questions about services, help when a family has received a letter denying or terminating in-home services, and step in when a care coordinator cannot be reached. Where a provider has violated a person's rights, the program states it will “assist in filing a grievance” and “work to resolve complaints.” That makes it a place to start before a problem has become a crisis — a missed visit, a confusing notice, or a care plan that no longer matches what your parent actually needs.

How to reach one

Call the Illinois Department on Aging's Senior HelpLine at 1-800-252-8966 (711 TRS), press 4 for “other services,” and ask for a Home Care Ombudsman. The department's page also lists a direct email, Aging.HCOProgram@illinois.gov, for the program itself.

What this program is not

Three limits are worth knowing before you call. It is built around the two eligibility routes above — a caregiver you found and pay directly, outside Medicaid or a waiver, is a different situation, covered in Is it safe to hire a caregiver directly in Illinois? It advocates and helps resolve a problem; it does not replace an emergency response — if your parent is in immediate danger, call 911 first. And it is a different program, with a different phone script, from the Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program that covers facility residents, so naming the right one when you call the Senior HelpLine gets you to the right advocate faster.
