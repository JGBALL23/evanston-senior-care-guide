How to report a problem at an Illinois care facility

Short answer

Two different state contacts, for two different jobs. The Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program, run by the Illinois Department on Aging, is a free, confidential, resident-directed advocate — call the Senior HelpLine at 1-800-252-8966 (711 TRS). The Illinois Department of Public Health's Central Complaint Registry is the state regulator that investigates facilities — call 800-252-4343, Monday–Friday 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. You do not have to choose just one.

Two kinds of help, and when to use each

Families searching for how to complain about a care community usually find one of two very different state contacts, and the difference matters. One is an advocate who works for the resident and tries to resolve the problem informally. The other is a regulator who investigates and can act against the facility's licence. Neither replaces the other, and you can use both at once.

The Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program

The Illinois Department on Aging describes its Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program as a “resident-directed advocacy program,” mandated by the federal Older Americans Act and the Illinois Act on Aging. Ombudsmen advocate for better care in Illinois long-term care facilities, help residents speak up for themselves, explain residents' rights, and investigate complaints to try to resolve them to the resident's satisfaction.

Two things are worth knowing before you call. First, the program states plainly that its work is directed by the resident — it is not a service a family member sets in motion and then runs. Second, it keeps information confidential and will not disclose it without the individual's permission. The department's page lists current and former residents, their friends and relatives, facility staff, and anyone considering placement as people who may contact an ombudsman, and the service is free.

To reach the program, call the Senior HelpLine at 1-800-252-8966 (711 TRS) or email Aging.SLTCOProgram@illinois.gov. The department's contact page also lists direct numbers for the State Long-Term Care Ombudsman and deputy staff, but a family with a new concern should start with the HelpLine rather than guessing which named contact to use.

The Illinois Department of Public Health's Central Complaint Registry

IDPH licenses and regulates health care facilities in Illinois, and its Central Complaint Registry is the channel for reporting a concern that may call for a state investigation rather than informal advocacy. The department's complaints page gives the hotline as 800-252-4343, with hours of Monday–Friday 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.

The same page states that for a long-term care complaint specifically, that same number reaches the department's Bureau of Long-Term Care. Besides the phone line, the page lists three other ways to file: online through the OHCR Portal, by fax at 217-524-8885, or by e-mail to DPH.CCR@illinois.gov using the Health Care Facilities Complaint Form. Written complaints can also go by mail to the Illinois Department of Public Health, Office of Health Care Regulation, Central Complaint Registry, 525 W. Jefferson St., Ground Floor, Springfield, IL 62761-0001.

IDPH says your identity is not disclosed to the facility, and you may file anonymously or leave contact information to receive written notice of the outcome. Describing who, what, when, where and how gives investigators the most to work with.

A number this guide will not publish

The Ombudsman Program's own page carries a separate navigation link reading “Report Abuse: 1-866-800-1409,” but the page itself never states what that line reaches or how it relates to the HelpLine number above it. Rather than guess at an unexplained number on a government page, this guide leaves it out. If you come across it elsewhere, confirm what it connects to with the Department on Aging before relying on it.

If someone is in immediate danger

None of the above is the right channel for an emergency. If a resident's life or safety is at risk right now, call 911 first and make the state reports afterward.
