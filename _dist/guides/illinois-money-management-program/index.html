Who can help my parent pay bills and manage money in Illinois?

Short answer

Yes, through a specific state programme — but only for two groups: a parent already approved for the Community Care Program, or one with a substantiated Adult Protective Services case, and only in the parts of Illinois a local Program Site actually covers. The Illinois Department on Aging's Volunteer Money Management Program trains, matches and monitors volunteers who budget, pay bills, and can be named a parent's Social Security representative payee. Its own current site list does not show a Program Site reaching Evanston Township — call the Senior HelpLine to check before assuming it is out of reach.

What the Volunteer Money Management Program actually does

The Illinois Department on Aging describes its Volunteer Money Management Program as “a protective service that assists low-income older persons who have difficulty managing their personal household budgets, paying bills”. Its own page lists what that covers in practice: “keeping track of banking records, intervening with creditors, completing medical forms”, “setting up a budget, writing checks, balancing a checkbook”, and “assisting with large purchases”.

This sits below a Power of Attorney and short of a court guardianship. Nobody signs away legal authority to make this work — a trained volunteer simply does the paperwork your parent can no longer keep up with, under a programme the state recruits, trains and monitors.

Knowing a volunteer bookkeeper exists is a different question from knowing whether your parent can still manage living at home at all.

Who qualifies — and where

The department's page gives exactly two eligibility paths. The programme is “available to Community Care Program (CCP) participants” — see Can my parent get paid help at home in Illinois? for what CCP itself requires — or to “individuals who have a substantiated Adult Protective Services (APS) case in certain areas of the State”. For what counts as a substantiated APS case, see What to do about suspected elder abuse or neglect at home in Illinois.

Both paths carry the same catch: “in certain areas of the State” is doing real work in that sentence. This is not a programme every Illinois county runs — it operates through a fixed list of contracted Program Sites, and whether one serves your parent's township decides whether any of this is actually available to them.

Volunteers, training, and acting as a Social Security representative payee

The department's page states that “Local Program Sites, contracted by the Illinois Department on Aging, recruit, select, train, match and monitor the volunteers”, and that the department itself “administers the program and monitors the local Program Sites”. Volunteers are matched to an older adult who needs help “organizing, reviewing, and paying bills”.

One sentence on the page matters more than the rest for a family wondering how a parent's Social Security or other federal benefit cheque gets managed: “Volunteers also serve as Representative Payees, persons designated by the Social Security Administration to manage the monthly benefits of certain beneficiaries.” That is the same role the Illinois Department on Aging's own resident-rights brochure warns a care facility may not take on “without your permission” — this programme is a vetted, state-monitored way to fill that role with someone other than the facility, a stranger, or nobody at all.

What this guide could not verify, and will not guess at

The department's page does not state what the programme costs a family, does not describe how a volunteer is screened beyond being “trained”, and does not explain what happens if a volunteer mishandles money beyond saying the department “monitors” Program Sites. This guide will not guess at a fee, a background-check standard, or a complaint process the page itself does not spell out. Ask the Program Site named for your area, or the Senior HelpLine, directly.

Is a Program Site actually near Evanston?

The department publishes the current roster as a PDF, “Find Your Local Program Site/Agency”, organised by the state's Planning and Service Areas. The copy this guide read, dated 22 October 2026, lists two Program Sites covering parts of Chicago's suburbs: Kenneth W. Young Centers for Elk Grove and Schaumburg Townships, and Oak Park Township Senior Services for Oak Park and River Forest Townships. Neither covers Evanston Township, and this guide found no separate listing for the City of Chicago or the rest of Cook County on that document.

That is a fact about one dated PDF, not a permanent answer. Program Site lists change, and the department's Service Locator tool — built for Care Coordination Units rather than this programme specifically — may turn up a more current answer than this guide's single reading of one document. Call the Senior HelpLine at 1-800-252-8966 (711 TRS) and ask directly whether a Program Site currently serves Evanston Township, rather than relying on either this guide or an out-of-date list found elsewhere.

How to find out, for your specific parent

Start with the Senior HelpLine at 1-800-252-8966 (711 TRS), or a local Care Coordination Unit found through the department's online Service Locator. Either can tell you whether your parent's situation — already on CCP, or with a substantiated APS case — qualifies, and whether a Program Site actually reaches where your parent lives, before you assume either answer yourself.
