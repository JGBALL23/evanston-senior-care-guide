What happened to my parent’s MMAI plan, and what is a FIDE SNP?

Short answer

If your parent has both Medicare and full Illinois Medicaid (“dually eligible”), the combined plan Illinois called MMAI ended on 31 December 2025. Illinois now offers FIDE SNPs (fully integrated dual eligible special needs plans), a type of Medicare Advantage plan that provides Medicare and Medicaid benefits through one managed care plan. MMAI members of Aetna, Humana, Meridian and Molina were moved automatically into those companies’ FIDE SNPs on 1 January 2026; Blue Cross Blue Shield did not offer one. Not every dually eligible person can join, and the annual Medicare Open Enrollment Period runs from 15 October to 7 December.

MMAI ended on 31 December 2025

Illinois HFS states: “The Illinois Medicare-Medicaid Alignment Initiative (MMAI) program will end December 31, 2025.” HFS says the reason is that “the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) is requiring states with a MMAI program to convert to the D-SNP model,” and that “FIDE SNPs will be the only type of D-SNP model available in Illinois”.

HFS describes a FIDE SNP as “a type of Medicare Advantage plan” that provides both Medicare and Medicaid benefits through one managed care plan, and says FIDE SNPs are the most like MMAI plans because “they provide the same level of care coordination”. It adds that “Medicaid wraps around Medicare by helping with Medicare premiums and cost sharing, and by covering some services that Medicare does not cover, such as long-term services and supports (LTSS).”

Knowing which plan now handles your parent’s Medicare and Medicaid coverage is a different question from knowing whether the care it arranges is still the right amount of help.

What happened to MMAI members

HFS’s enrolment page says that for “Members of an MMAI plan offered by Aetna, Humana, Meridian, and Molina,” “CMS will automatically enroll these members in the Illinois FIDE SNP offered by these companies” with a start date of 1 January 2026. Those members did not need to do anything, and could choose other coverage instead.

Blue Cross Blue Shield was different: “Blue Cross Blue Shield will not be offering a FIDE SNP for 2026.” HFS says BCBS MMAI members who made no choice before 31 December 2025 were automatically enrolled by CMS in Original Medicare with a Medicare drug plan from 1 January 2026. For their Medicaid benefits, members receiving long-term services and supports were automatically enrolled in the BCBS HealthChoice Illinois MLTSS plan, and other members receive services in the HFS fee-for-service program. HFS also says a BCBS letter dated 3 November 2025, which told members they could stay in BCBS MMAI, “should not have been sent by BCBS.”

Who can join a FIDE SNP, and who cannot

HFS’s eligibility list requires “Receiving full Illinois Medicaid benefits” in the Aid to the Aged, Blind, and Disabled (AABD) or FamilyCare category, being 21 or older at enrolment, having Medicare Part A and Part B, living in the plan’s service area, and being a United States citizen or lawfully present in the United States.

Medicare itself is one of those conditions, and the 2025 federal law narrowed it for noncitizens. HFS states: “For new Medicare applicants on or after July 4, 2025, only these noncitizens can be eligible”: lawful permanent residents (green card holders), Cubans or Haitians with a range of current or past statuses, and people from Micronesia, Palau or the Marshall Islands living in the U.S. It adds: “If you were already enrolled in Medicare as of July 4, 2025, you can keep your coverage until January 4, 2027, when it ends.” A parent who is lawfully present under another status may therefore lose Medicare, and with it a FIDE SNP, on that date.

HFS says a person is not eligible if they are under 21, receive developmental disability institutional services or take part in the HCBS waiver for Adults with Developmental Disabilities, are “In the Medicaid Spend-down population,” are in the Illinois Medicaid Breast and Cervical Cancer program, are enrolled in partial-benefit Medicaid programs, or receive Comprehensive Third Party Insurance. If your parent qualifies for Medicaid through spend-down, explained in How Illinois Medicaid pays for long-term care, and what spend-down means, a FIDE SNP is not open to them.

If your parent is in a nursing home or on a waiver

Care in a nursing facility, or under one of Illinois’s Home and Community Based Services waivers, falls under what HFS calls long-term services and supports (LTSS); its managed care page says dual-eligible adults receiving certain of these services are “referred to as the MLTSS population”. If your parent is in a FIDE SNP, those Medicaid services come through that one plan, which HFS says provides both Medicare and Medicaid benefits. For dually eligible members who do not choose a FIDE SNP, HFS’s enrolment page says members receiving LTSS “must be enrolled in a HealthChoice IL Managed Long-Term Services and Support (MLTSS) plan” for those services, and adds: “Currently, to switch plans, members must call the HFS Client Enrollment Broker at 1-877-912-8880.”

This guide has not researched HealthChoice Illinois MLTSS plans themselves. Ask your parent’s care coordinator, or the HFS Client Enrollment Broker, which plan your parent is in now and what changing it would mean.

How and when to enrol or switch

HFS’s enrolment page names three ways to enrol in a FIDE SNP: use the “Find Plans Now” tool on Medicare.gov and select “Medicaid” when asked if you get help with your Medicare health or drug costs; call the plan or visit its website; or call 1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227), TTY 1-877-486-2048.

The Medicare Open Enrollment Period runs “October 15 - December 7 of each year,” and coverage chosen then starts on 1 January, as long as the plan gets the request by 7 December. Outside that window, HFS says “members eligible for a FIDE SNP can enroll once-per-month in a FIDE SNP using the Integrated Care SEP,” with coverage starting the first of the month after the plan gets the request. A separate Dual/LIS special enrolment period lets a dually eligible member go back to Original Medicare with a standalone prescription drug plan, or switch drug plans, once a month. HFS says these special enrolment periods “won’t allow enrollment into non-D-SNP Medicare Advantage Plans.”

For free help choosing a plan, see Who can help my parent choose a Medicare plan for free in Illinois?

What this guide could not verify

HFS’s pages give no premium, copay or other dollar figure for a FIDE SNP, so this guide does not publish one. Compare plans’ costs on Medicare.gov or with the plan itself. Several HFS pages still describe the change in the future tense, as written before it took effect; this guide reports what they say happened on 1 January 2026.
