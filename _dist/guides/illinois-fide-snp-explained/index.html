What happened to my parent’s MMAI plan, and what is a FIDE SNP?

Short answer

If your parent is enrolled in both Medicare and Medicaid (“dually eligible”) in Illinois, the plan that used to be called MMAI ended on 31 December 2025. Illinois HFS states that most MMAI members were automatically enrolled in a Fully Integrated Dual Eligible Special Needs Plan (FIDE SNP) run by the same company, starting 1 January 2026 — except Blue Cross Blue Shield, which is not offering one for 2026. A FIDE SNP combines Medicare and Medicaid benefits in a single plan, the same way MMAI did. Some people cannot enrol in one at all, and anyone already in a nursing home or a home care waiver who wants to leave has a different route than everyone else.

MMAI ended on 31 December 2025

Illinois HFS states plainly: “The Illinois Medicare-Medicaid Alignment Initiative (MMAI) program will end December 31, 2025.” MMAI was the name Illinois used for over a decade for a managed-care plan that combined Medicare and Medicaid coverage for people eligible for both. HFS says the change happened because the federal Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) “is requiring states with a MMAI program to convert to the D-SNP model.”

The replacement has a specific name, and HFS is explicit that it is the only version available here: “FIDE SNPs will be the only type of D-SNP model available in Illinois.” HFS describes a Fully Integrated Dual Eligible Special Needs Plan, or FIDE SNP, as “a type of Medicare Advantage plan that provides both Medicare and Medicaid benefits through a single managed care plan” — and says FIDE SNPs were chosen as the closest match to MMAI because they “provide the same level of care coordination.”

Knowing which FIDE SNP plan now administers your parent’s Medicare and Medicaid coverage is a different question from knowing whether the care it arranges is still the right amount of help.

What moved automatically, and who has to choose instead

What happened next depended on where your parent’s coverage stood on 31 December 2025. HFS states that CMS “will automatically enroll” members of the four MMAI companies — Aetna, Humana, Meridian and Molina — “in the Illinois FIDE SNP offered by these companies,” effective 1 January 2026. If your parent’s MMAI plan was with one of those four, nothing had to be chosen for coverage to continue, under the same company, as a FIDE SNP.

Blue Cross Blue Shield is the one exception named on the page, and it is worth knowing about specifically: “Blue Cross Blue Shield will not be offering a FIDE SNP for 2026.” HFS also flags a letter some families may have already seen and should disregard: a “11/3/2025 BCBS letter” telling members they could stay on BCBS MMAI from 1 January 2026 “should not have been sent,” and HFS states those members “do not have the opportunity to enroll in BCBS MMAI effective 1/1/2026.” If your parent was on BCBS MMAI, that letter was wrong, and a different plan had to be chosen.

Anyone who was dually eligible but not already in MMAI had a separate, earlier window: HFS states these members “can enroll in any Illinois FIDE SNP or other forms of coverage beginning October 15, 2025.”

Who cannot enrol in a FIDE SNP at all

A FIDE SNP is not open to every Illinois Medicaid recipient, even a dually eligible one. HFS’s own eligibility list requires “receiving full Illinois Medicaid benefits” in the AABD or FamilyCare category, being at least 21, having both Medicare Part A and Part B, living in the plan’s service area, and being a citizen or lawfully present in the US.

HFS then names several specific exclusions families researching long-term care should read carefully: someone is not eligible for a FIDE SNP if they are under 21, receive developmental disability institutional services or are in the related home and community-based services waiver, are in the Medicaid spend-down population, are in the Illinois Breast and Cervical Cancer program, are in partial-benefit Medicaid, or have Comprehensive Third Party Insurance. If your parent reached Medicaid eligibility through spend-down — covered in How Illinois Medicaid pays for long-term care, and what spend-down means — a FIDE SNP is not an option for them regardless of what any letter about MMAI might have implied.

If your parent is in a nursing home or a home care waiver

HFS’s managed-care page for clients draws a specific line for this site’s readers: members “in a nursing facility or in certain Home and Community Based Services waivers” who want to leave MMAI do not simply pick another Medicare plan — they “must move to the HealthChoice Illinois program for MLTSS members instead.” MLTSS stands for Medicaid Long Term Services and Supports. That is a different programme from the FIDE SNP enrolment process described above, and this guide did not research HealthChoice Illinois MLTSS itself — ask your parent’s care coordinator or the CCU handling their waiver which route actually applies before assuming either one.

How to enrol, switch, or opt out

HFS’s enrolment page points families toward three channels, not one. The first is Medicare.gov itself: use its “Find Plans Now” tool, entering a zip code and selecting Medicaid when asked about help with costs, to compare the FIDE SNPs available there. The second is contacting a plan directly or visiting its website. The third is calling Medicare directly at 1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227), TTY 1-877-486-2048.

Three enrolment windows govern timing. The annual Medicare Open Enrollment Period runs “October 15 - December 7 of each year,” with coverage starting the following 1 January. Outside that window, someone eligible for a FIDE SNP “can enroll once-per-month in a FIDE SNP using the Integrated Care SEP.” A separate Dual/LIS SEP lets a member switch to Original Medicare with a standalone drug plan, or between standalone drug plans, once a month — but HFS states neither special enrolment period “will allow enrollment into non-D-SNP Medicare Advantage Plans.” One rule applies regardless of which window is used: “Members cannot opt out of Medicare coverage.”

For changing which Medicaid managed care plan a client is in, rather than the Medicare side, HFS's enrolment page gives a separate number: the HFS Client Enrollment Broker at 1-877-912-8880.

What this guide could not verify

Three things this guide read about but will not state as settled. First, cost: neither HFS page this guide fetched gives a premium, copay or dollar figure for a FIDE SNP — the pages describe Medicaid as “helping with Medicare premiums and cost sharing” for a dually eligible member without stating an amount, so this guide will not guess one. Second, a search result surfaced an email address for HFS questions about this change; this guide could not find that address stated on any HFS page it actually fetched and read, so it is not reprinted here — use the verified phone numbers above instead. Third, this guide read a January 2026 HFS notice describing enrolment data problems during the changeover that a manual reconciliation process was being built to fix; it did not find a later HFS page confirming that fix is complete, so if your parent’s plan still looks wrong months later, that is worth raising directly with HFS or the plan rather than assuming it will resolve itself.
