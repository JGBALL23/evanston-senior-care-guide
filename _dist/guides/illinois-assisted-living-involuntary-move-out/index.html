Can an Illinois assisted living make your parent move out, and how much notice must it give?

Short answer

Only through a formal process — not simply by being asked to leave. The Illinois Department of Public Health's own Involuntary Termination of Residency form states that a 30-day prior written notice must go to the resident, the resident’s representative, or both, and to the long-term care ombudsman, and that a resident “has the right to continue to reside in the establishment until a decision is rendered.” The Illinois Department on Aging’s residents’ rights brochure, which it says is “appropriate for nursing homes and assisted living”, lists four reasons a facility can make a resident leave — see below.

What IDPH's own form requires before a move-out

When a family searches for whether an Illinois assisted living or shared housing establishment can make a resident leave, the most direct answer is the state's own paperwork for it. The Illinois Department of Public Health (IDPH) publishes an Assisted Living and Shared Housing Involuntary Termination of Residency form, and the form itself states the rule in one sentence: “A 30-day prior written notice must be provided to the resident, resident’s representative, or both, and to the long-term care ombudsman.” The same form adds: “The establishment must notify the Illinois Department of Public Health when it initiates the termination process.” So a termination is not a private matter between the resident and the establishment; the state is notified too.

The form itself has a line the establishment must fill in, labelled “Reason for Residency Termination”. The Department on Aging’s residents’ rights brochure adds that the written notice must include why the resident is being asked to move.

The right to appeal, and what happens while you wait

The same IDPH form sets out exactly how a resident contests a termination: by calling IDPH's Division of Assisted Living at 217-782-2448, or by sending a signed Appeal Hearing Request form to IDPH. The accompanying Hearing Request form gives the deadline in capital letters: “Please submit completed forms within 10 DAYS after receiving the Involuntary Termination of Residency form from the facility.”

Filing on time matters, because of what the form says happens next: “The resident has the right to continue to reside in the establishment until a decision is rendered.” The Hearing Request form can be mailed or faxed to 217-557-2432, to the Illinois Department of Public Health, Division of Assisted Living, 525 West Jefferson Street, 5th Floor, Springfield, Illinois 62761. The termination form also has a line naming the person at the establishment who will assist with relocation.

What reasons can be given

The question families actually ask is broader than notice periods: can an establishment end a stay because a parent's care needs have grown? The Illinois Department on Aging's brochure Residents’ Rights for People in Long-Term Care Facilities, which the Department's residents' rights page describes as “appropriate for nursing homes and assisted living”, says a facility can make a resident leave because the bill has not been paid, the resident is dangerous to themselves or others, their medical needs cannot be met, or the facility closes.

The full assisted living rules are in Part 295 of the Illinois Administrative Code, which IDPH's Assisted Living page links to as “Assisted Living and Shared Housing - 295 Administrative Code”.

Practically, that means: ask the establishment to point to the specific reason it wrote on your parent's notice, and call IDPH's Division of Assisted Living (217-782-2448) or the Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program to ask whether that reason matches a recognised ground for termination before deciding whether to appeal.

Your parent's broader rights as a resident

The Illinois Department on Aging's page on residents' rights in long-term care facilities states that care facility residents keep certain rights, protections and privileges under both Illinois and federal law. The Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program — which the involuntary termination notice must also be sent to — is reached by calling the Department on Aging’s Senior HelpLine at 1-800-252-8966 (711 TRS), and the Department lists involuntary transfers or discharges among the reasons to contact an ombudsman.
