Can an Illinois assisted living make your parent move out, and how much notice must it give?

Short answer

Only through a formal process — not simply by being asked to leave. The Illinois Department of Public Health's own Involuntary Termination of Residency form states that a 30-day prior written notice must go to the resident, the resident’s representative, or both, and to the long-term care ombudsman, and that a resident “has the right to continue to reside in the establishment until a decision is rendered” on a timely appeal. This guide could not confirm, from a government page it could read and cite, the specific list of situations that legally justify a termination — see below for why, and who to ask.

What IDPH's own form requires before a move-out

When a family searches for whether an Illinois assisted living or shared housing establishment can make a resident leave, the most direct answer is the state's own paperwork for it. The Illinois Department of Public Health (IDPH) publishes an Assisted Living and Shared Housing Involuntary Termination of Residency form, and the form itself states the rule in one sentence: “A 30-day prior written notice must be provided to the resident, resident’s representative, or both, and to the long-term care ombudsman.” The same form adds: “The establishment must notify the Illinois Department of Public Health when it initiates the termination process.” So a termination is not a private matter between the resident and the establishment; the state is notified too.

The form itself has a line the establishment must fill in, labelled “Reason for Residency Termination”. IDPH's form does not leave that blank or optional — a reason has to be stated in writing, on the notice your parent or their representative receives.

The right to appeal, and what happens while you wait

The same IDPH form sets out exactly how a resident contests a termination: by calling IDPH's Division of Assisted Living at 217-782-2448, or by sending a signed Appeal Hearing Request form to IDPH. The accompanying Hearing Request form gives the deadline in capital letters: “Please submit completed forms within 10 DAYS after receiving the Involuntary Termination of Residency form from the facility.”

Filing on time matters, because of what the form says happens next: “The resident has the right to continue to reside in the establishment until a decision is rendered.” An appeal filed within the 10-day window does not just register a complaint — it keeps your parent in place while IDPH decides. The Hearing Request form can be mailed or faxed to 217-557-2432, to the Illinois Department of Public Health, Division of Assisted Living, 525 West Jefferson Street, 5th Floor, Springfield, Illinois 62761. The termination form also requires the establishment to name a specific staff person who will assist with relocation, so a family is not left to figure out logistics with no one assigned to help.

What this guide could not verify

The question families actually ask is broader than notice periods: can an establishment legally end a stay simply because a parent's care needs have grown? This guide searched repeatedly for an IDPH or Illinois Department on Aging page, on an allowed government host, that sets out the specific grounds for an involuntary termination — comparable to the four stated reasons the Department on Aging publishes for nursing homes (see the link below). It did not find one. The detailed criteria appear to live in the Illinois Administrative Code, but that text is published on the Illinois General Assembly's website, which is a separate domain from the Illinois government sites this library cites, so this guide will not quote criteria it read there.

Practically, that means: ask the establishment to point to the specific reason it wrote on your parent's notice, and call IDPH's Division of Assisted Living (217-782-2448) or the Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program to ask whether that reason matches a recognised ground for termination before deciding whether to appeal. An elder law attorney can also review the notice against the current regulations directly.

Your parent's broader rights as a resident

The Illinois Department on Aging's page on residents' rights in long-term care facilities states that care facility residents keep certain rights, protections and privileges under both Illinois and federal law. The Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program — which the involuntary termination notice must also be sent to — is reachable through the Senior HelpLine at 1-800-252-8966 (711 TRS), and can advocate for your parent during an appeal, not only after one has already gone wrong.
