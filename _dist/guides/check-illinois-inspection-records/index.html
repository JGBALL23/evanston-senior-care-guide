How to check an Illinois care community's inspection record

Short answer

It depends on what each part of the community is licensed as. For a nursing home, Medicare Care Compare publishes inspection, staffing and quality data. The Illinois Department of Public Health publishes quarterly reports of the nursing home violations it has acted on. For assisted living, this guide found no public online lookup, so ask the community for its most recent state survey. Illinois rules require it to make that available for review.

Start with what each part is licensed as

Illinois licenses nursing homes and assisted living under different laws. Their records are kept in different places. One campus can hold more than one licence. So first ask what each part of a community is licensed as. The answer tells you where to look.

What you are looking for is not a perfect record — almost nobody has one. You are looking for patterns. A single citation three years ago that was corrected is noise. The same category of problem appearing in consecutive inspections is a signal.

An inspection record tells you how well a community does its job. It cannot tell you whether that is the job your parent needs doing in the first place.

For a nursing home, check Medicare Care Compare

Medicare Care Compare covers Medicare-certified nursing homes. Besides inspection results, it publishes reported staffing levels, including registered nurse hours per resident per day, and staff turnover.

Staffing is the measure most closely tied to daily experience, and turnover is worth understanding rather than predicting. A turnover rate describes the staff of a building over a year. It does not tell you how often one resident will meet someone new, and this guide will not pretend otherwise — a high-turnover community can still have a stable team on one floor, and a low-turnover one can rotate your parent’s aide weekly.

What the number is genuinely useful for is as a question, not a verdict. Bring it to the visit. Ask who would be caring for your parent on a weekday morning, how long those specific people have worked there, how shifts are assigned, and how often agency staff fill in. Compare the answers to the published figure. A community whose answers match its data has told you something real; so has one where they do not.

Note the scope limit honestly: Care Compare covers nursing homes. Assisted living is licensed by the state rather than Medicare-certified, so Care Compare has no record of it. The Evanston section below shows where its record is.

How to check an Evanston community's record

Ask each Evanston community you are considering what each part of it is licensed as. Then check each part where its record is kept.

  • Nursing care. This means a nursing home, or the nursing part of a larger campus. Look it up on Medicare Care Compare. Then open the most recent of the Illinois Department of Public Health’s quarterly reports of nursing home violators and search the page for “Evanston”. Each entry gives a facility’s name and address, the survey date, the type of each violation and its fine. On your visit, ask to see the inspection reports. Illinois requires a nursing home to keep every state inspection report from the past five years, and every state or court order, for public inspection.
  • Assisted living. The Illinois Department of Public Health licenses it, and the law calls for an unannounced state visit to each establishment every year. This guide found no public online lookup of the results. So ask the community. Illinois rules require it to make its most recent state survey results, and any statement of correction still in effect, available for review. Ask how each finding was corrected.
  • Memory care. Ask which licence it operates under, and check that record as above. Then ask for the written dementia-care disclosure. Illinois requires a facility with an Alzheimer’s special care unit to set out certain facts in writing for a potential resident or their representative. They include its admission and discharge procedures, its minimum and maximum staffing ratios, its activities, the family’s role and its costs.
  • Independent living. This site found no Illinois care licence for it, so it has no state inspection record of this kind. If the campus also has assisted living or nursing care your parent might need later, check those records now. The independent living guide explains why.

The same steps work anywhere in Illinois. Only the town you search for changes.

What the Illinois nursing home violation types mean

The quarterly reports label each violation with a type. The Nursing Home Care Act defines four:

  • Type AA: a violation that proximately caused a resident’s death.
  • Type A: one that created a substantial probability of death or serious physical or mental harm to a resident, or that caused actual physical or mental harm.
  • Type B: one more likely than not to cause more than minimal physical or mental harm.
  • Type C: one with a substantial probability of causing less than minimal physical or mental harm.

These four types come from the nursing home law. They do not describe assisted living findings.

Reading a record without panicking

  • Check the date. A finding from four years ago under different management is weaker evidence than one from last quarter.
  • Check severity. Citations are scaled. A paperwork deficiency is not a resident-harm finding. For a nursing home, the violation type tells you which you are reading.
  • Check whether it recurred. Correction is normal. Repetition is the problem.
  • Read absence carefully. A community missing from one quarter’s report had no state action listed that quarter. That is not the same as a clean record.
  • Take it with you. Print the record and ask the community about a specific finding on your visit. How they answer tells you as much as the finding did.
