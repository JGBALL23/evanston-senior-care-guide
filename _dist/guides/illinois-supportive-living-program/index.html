Illinois Supportive Living Program, explained

Short answer

The Supportive Living Program is how Illinois Medicaid pays for assisted-living-style care. It covers the cost of services in an approved Supportive Living facility. The resident pays a state-set room and board charge, plus a group care credit calculated from their own income, and keeps a personal needs allowance of $120 a month.

What it is, and what it is not

A Supportive Living facility is not the same thing as a private-pay assisted living community, and it is not a nursing home. It is a separate category of setting, approved by the state, where Medicaid pays for the care a resident receives.

Not every assisted living community in Illinois participates. A community can be excellent and still not be a Supportive Living facility. Ask directly, early, because it decides whether a place is financially possible at all.

Supportive Living is one setting among several, and it only helps if it is the setting your parent needs. The programme cannot tell you whether it is.

How the money actually works

Expect two charges, not one. The first is room and board, which stays the resident's responsibility. Illinois sets it statewide and adjusts it each year with the Social Security cost-of-living increase: $874 a month for single occupancy and $625.50 for double occupancy, effective 1 January 2026.

The second is the group care credit, also called patient liability. It is not a fixed price and it is not the same for two residents of the same building. The state calculates it from that resident's own countable income after allowable deductions — health insurance premiums, certain medical expenses, the room and board portion, and the $120 personal needs allowance. Whatever remains is paid monthly toward the cost of care, and Medicaid covers the rest.

So there is no sum on this page that produces your parent's figure, and any site that offers you one is guessing. The amount owed is determined by the state for that individual, from documents you have not filed yet. What you can know in advance is the method, the two charges, and the one thing that is fixed: the $120 personal needs allowance a resident keeps for everything personal — clothing, haircuts, a phone, gifts for grandchildren. Most residents owe both charges; a resident avoids the group care credit only when their income equals the SSI amount.

Questions worth asking before you apply

  • Is this specific community an approved Supportive Living facility?
  • Is there a waiting list, and how long is it in practice?
  • What is the room and board amount at this facility?
  • What happens if my parent's needs exceed what this setting provides?
  • Who at the facility helps with the application, and what do we need to gather first?

Eligibility rules, income tests and asset limits change, and they are decided by the state rather than by any community. Confirm current figures with Illinois HFS or a benefits counsellor before making any financial decision. This guide explains how the programme works; it is not an eligibility determination.
