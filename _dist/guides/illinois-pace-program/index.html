What is PACE, and can a parent in Evanston use it?

Short answer

Not yet, if the question is Evanston specifically. PACE — the Program of All-Inclusive Care for the Elderly — is a joint Medicare and Medicaid programme that pays for full medical care, prescription drugs, adult day service and more so that an older adult who would otherwise need a nursing home can stay in the community. Illinois HFS runs it in five regions, and its own zip-code tool for finding a local PACE organisation does not list any of Evanston's residential zip codes (60201, 60202, 60203, 60208) as of this review. The programme is still rolling out, so this guide shows where it operates today and how to check again yourself.

What PACE actually is

Illinois HFS describes PACE as “comprehensive health services for seniors living in the community” who “would otherwise qualify to live in a nursing facility.” Medicare.gov sets out four conditions a person has to meet: be at least 55; live in the service area of a PACE organisation, since PACE is only available in some states that offer it under Medicaid, and then only in the specific local areas a PACE organisation actually covers; need a nursing-home level of care, which the state certifies; and be able to live safely in the community with PACE's help.

Each PACE organisation runs a physical centre — HFS describes a primary care clinic together with space for recreation, therapy, socialising, personal care and dining — and is responsible for coordinating a participant's care everywhere it happens: at that centre, at home, or in a hospital or long-term care admission when one is needed. That round-the-clock coordination, not any single service on the list, is what makes PACE different from the home-care and day-service programmes this site already covers.

PACE is worth understanding even where it does not yet reach, because the question it answers — can my parent get full medical and personal care without leaving home — is the same question every setting on this site is trying to answer.

What it covers, and what it costs

Medicare.gov says PACE covers all Medicare- and Medicaid-covered care, plus anything else a participant's own PACE care team decides they need. The page names prescription drugs, adult day care with meals and recreational therapy, and transportation to the PACE centre and to medical appointments among the services included.

Cost depends on what the participant already has. With Medicaid, there is no monthly premium for the long-term-care part of the benefit. With Medicare but no Medicaid, cost depends on whether the person has Part A and/or Part B, and includes a premium for the long-term-care portion plus a separate Part D drug premium. With neither, a person can pay the PACE organisation's premium directly. Whichever of those applies, Medicare.gov states that no participant pays a deductible, copayment or coinsurance for any drug, service or care their PACE team approves.

One trade-off worth knowing before applying: PACE replaces other Medicare drug coverage rather than sitting alongside it. Medicare.gov says that enrolling in a separate Medicare drug plan while in PACE gets a participant disenrolled from PACE — the two cannot run together.

Where PACE operates in Illinois today

HFS names five Illinois regions: West Chicago, South Chicago, Southern Cook County, Peoria and East St. Louis. Its own page says PACE services “began becoming available in some regions beginning mid-2024” — this is a recent, rolling launch, not a programme that has covered the whole state for years.

HFS's designated-organisations page names who runs each region: Kinship PACE of Illinois, LLC and Lawndale Christian Health Center for West Side Chicago; Habitat Health at Annie's Place and Esperanza Health Centers for South Side Chicago; BoldAge PACE Illinois for Southern Cook County; OSF HealthCare System for Peoria; and Habitat Health at Stella's Place for East St. Louis. The page gives no launch date for any of them, and describes Kinship specifically as “a new entrant into the Illinois health care delivery system” — so a family calling any of these should ask directly whether it is actually taking participants yet, rather than assume active service from being named on the list.

HFS's own zip-code search is how the state tells a family whether PACE reaches them, and the tool itself warns up front that PACE “may not be available in your area.” This guide checked it directly: none of Evanston's residential zip codes — 60201, 60202, 60203 and 60208 — appear on that list. Southern Cook County is the nearest named region, but its zip-code listing does not reach Evanston.

A figure this guide will not guess at

Families will want to know what the Medicare-only premium actually costs. Medicare.gov states plainly that it depends on whether a person has Part A and/or Part B, and that a separate Part D premium applies too — but the page gives no dollar figure or formula for either one, anywhere this guide could find. A premium that varies by organisation and by an individual's own Medicare status is not a number this guide can publish responsibly, so it will not estimate one. The page is also silent on what happens to a separate Medigap policy if someone joins PACE. Ask the specific PACE organisation covering your area, before assuming a cost in either direction.

If PACE is not reachable from Evanston yet

Because the zip-code tool above is literally how the state keeps this current, and the programme is explicitly still expanding region by region, it is worth re-checking that page yourself every so often rather than treating today's answer as permanent — a page written this month can be out of date by next year in a way a static fact rarely is.

In the meantime, two Illinois programmes already reach Evanston. For paid help at home — homemaker service, adult day service, emergency response — see Can my parent get paid help at home in Illinois? For Medicaid-funded care in an assisted-living-style setting, which is not limited to PACE's five regions, see Illinois Supportive Living Program, explained. Neither one coordinates care as tightly as PACE is designed to, but both are available to an Evanston family now rather than on a future expansion.
