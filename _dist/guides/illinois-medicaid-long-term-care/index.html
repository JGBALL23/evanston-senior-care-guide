How Illinois Medicaid pays for long-term care, and what spend-down means

Short answer

Yes, through the state's Medical Assistance Program. Illinois HFS states plainly that “payment for long-term nursing home services may be available from the state of Illinois.” To get it, you apply for medical benefits through the Department of Human Services (DHS) and get a needs screening through the Department on Aging or DHS. If your income or assets are above the limit, you may still qualify through spenddown — a program HFS describes as working “a little like an insurance deductible,” where medical bills above a set monthly amount count toward eligibility.

What Illinois Medicaid actually pays for

Medicaid is not one single long-term-care benefit. The Illinois Medical Assistance Program reimburses long-term care facilities for specific categories of service, which HFS lists as: skilled nursing facility services, intermediate care facility services, intermediate care for people with developmental disabilities (including specialized living centers), pediatric skilled nursing, and supportive living facility services.

In plain terms, that spans a licensed nursing home at one end and the state's assisted-living-style Supportive Living Program at the other. They are not interchangeable, and not every building accepts every category. Illinois Supportive Living Program, explained covers that middle option in its own right, and Illinois also pays for care at home through the Community Care Program and the Persons who are Elderly waiver rather than in a facility at all.

Working out who pays is a separate problem from working out what your parent needs paying for. Most families end up doing both, and the second one usually comes first.

How to apply

HFS sets out two separate steps, and both have to happen. You apply for medical benefits through the Department of Human Services (DHS), and you obtain a needs screening through the Department on Aging (DoA) or DHS. One is the financial side of eligibility; the other confirms the person actually needs the level of care being paid for. HFS points applicants to its HFS 3191 brochure for the detailed eligibility steps.

This is a state process run by caseworkers, not an online form you fill in once and forget. Expect to provide income and asset documentation, and expect a caseworker, not a website, to tell you exactly what is required in your parent's case.

What “spend-down” actually means

Spend-down exists for people who would otherwise be turned away for having too much income or too many assets. HFS describes it this way: “Spenddown works a little like an insurance deductible.” Each month, you are responsible for the cost of medical care up to a set amount, based on your income and assets, before Medicaid coverage for that month applies.

You do not necessarily have to pay that amount out of pocket in cash. HFS says a person can meet the monthly spenddown by showing a DHS caseworker unpaid medical bills — the bills themselves count toward the figure. Some recipients can also use “pay-in” spenddown, paying the amount directly to HFS instead of submitting bills; HFS describes this option as available to people enrolled in spenddown who are aged, blind, or have a disability and live in the community.

This is the detail families miss: nursing home care is expensive enough that the monthly spend-down amount for someone in a facility is often met immediately by the facility's own bill. Spenddown is frequently the route by which a long nursing home stay ends up Medicaid-covered even when the same person would not have qualified for Medicaid the month before they were admitted.

Protecting a spouse who stays at home

Federal and state rules exist specifically so that one spouse entering a nursing home does not force the other into poverty. Illinois HFS publishes updated dollar figures for this every year, and for 2026, effective 1 January 2026, it sets two of them.

Neither figure is an amount anyone is promised. Both are ceilings. HFS calls each one a standard and a maximum, and in both cases the amount that actually applies to a particular couple is worked out by subtracting what the at-home spouse already has.

The Community Spouse Resource Allowance standard is $143,172. HFS describes it as the maximum amount of resources a resident may transfer to a community spouse, or to another for that spouse's sole benefit, and states that the actual amount a resident may transfer is determined by deducting nonexempt resources of the community spouse from the standard. So a spouse who already holds countable resources of their own can transfer less than $143,172 — possibly much less.

The Community Spouse Maintenance Needs Allowance standard is $4,066.50 a month. HFS describes it as the maximum monthly income a resident may give to a community spouse, and states that the actual amount a resident may give is determined by deducting any gross income of the community spouse from the standard. A spouse with their own pension or Social Security therefore receives the difference, not the full $4,066.50.

This guide deliberately does not do that subtraction for you. It depends on which of a household's resources and income the state counts and which it exempts, and that determination belongs to the DHS caseworker, not to a website.

Both standards change every year, and HFS reissues the notice each time. Do not rely on a figure copied from any article, including this one, once a year has passed — check the current HFS notice or ask the DHS caseworker handling the case.

A figure this guide will not publish

Illinois law sets a monthly personal needs allowance for Medicaid nursing facility residents — a small amount of their own income protected for personal spending rather than put toward the cost of care. This guide went looking for the current figure on the HFS public notice that sets it, and could not read a verifiable number off that document. Rather than repeat a figure picked up secondhand, this guide leaves it out. Ask the nursing facility's business office or the DHS caseworker what the current personal needs allowance is before assuming any number you have seen elsewhere.
