What assisted living actually costs in Evanston and Illinois

Short answer

The median cost of assisted living in Illinois is $6,219 a month, according to the CareScout 2025 Cost of Care Survey. That number is a starting point, not a quote. What a family actually pays depends on the level of care assessed, whether a second person is living in the apartment, and a one-time community fee that the monthly figure never includes.

What the median actually measures

A median is the middle of a range. Half of Illinois communities charge more than $6,219 a month and half charge less. It is a statewide figure, not an Evanston figure. CareScout publishes no separate median for Evanston or for the north-shore suburbs, so this guide will not tell you how local prices compare — it does not know. Treat the statewide median as the number that tells you whether a quote is roughly normal, not as the number you will pay.

For comparison, the same survey puts the Illinois nursing home median at $8,304 a month for a semi-private room and $9,216 for a private room. Those are different levels of care, not different prices for the same thing.

There is no Illinois median published for independent living, and this guide does not invent one. Where a reliable figure does not exist, saying so is more useful than filling the gap.

A price only means something once you know which kind of care you are pricing. The same parent can be quoted wildly different monthly figures depending on the level of care a community assesses them into.

The four costs that surprise families

A community fee. A one-time charge at move-in, separate from rent. Ask for the amount in writing and ask whether any part of it is refundable if your parent leaves within a few months.

Care levels. Most communities assess a resident and assign a care tier that is billed on top of the base rate. The tier can change after a fall, a hospital stay, or a medication change. Ask what each tier costs, what moves someone between tiers, and who decides.

A second person. If both parents move in, there is almost always a second-occupant fee. It is rarely on the brochure.

The annual increase. Ask what the rate rose by in each of the last three years. A community that cannot answer that question has told you something.

What this means in Evanston

No survey we know of publishes an assisted living median for Evanston itself. The figures you will find are usually one of three kinds. They disagree because they measure different things, and none of them is your parent’s bill.

A statewide survey median. The $6,219 figure above is CareScout’s 2025 median monthly assisted living rate for a private one-bedroom in Illinois, from rates collected July to November 2025. Where a community quoted a range, from basic care to more substantial care, CareScout used the midpoint. It noted separately whether a community charges a non-refundable community or entrance fee. Its PDF tables are statewide and its regional figures follow metropolitan areas, so none covers Evanston alone.

A website’s estimate. Listing and referral websites publish their own “Evanston” figures. On 4 November 2026 we checked four that come up in a web search for Evanston assisted living costs. None explained how its figure was calculated. All four counted communities in other towns, including Chicago, as Evanston results. One is run by CareScout, but its “Evanston area” average is not one of the survey’s published figures.

A starting price. A “from” or “starting at” price is the lowest price shown, sometimes for one apartment type. It shows where prices begin, not what your parent’s apartment and care will cost.

The figure that counts is in the contract. Before anyone moves into a licensed assisted living establishment, Illinois requires a comprehensive assessment. It is repeated at least once a year and after a significant change in condition. The service plan built from it is a basis for the contract.

The contract must state the base rate and what it includes. It must describe the services that cost extra and set out their fees. It must also say whether there is a community fee, how much it is and whether it is refundable. The resident or their representative must get a complete copy, including later changes.

Once the assessment is done and the community has proposed a service plan, ask it for these figures in writing:

  • the base rate for the apartment your parent would actually take
  • the fee for each extra service your parent will use, including those in the service plan
  • any second-person fee
  • the community fee, and whether any of it is refundable

Convert the first three to monthly amounts and add them up. That total is the figure to compare between communities. Ask when the community fee is due.

The statewide median is only a rough check against that total. The survey used a midpoint wherever a community quoted a range of care prices, and it does not itemise charges.

A listing website’s results are not an official list. The Illinois Department of Public Health licenses assisted living and publishes a list of assisted living and shared housing establishments. How to check an Illinois care community’s inspection record explains how to look up each one’s record.

If paying privately is not realistic

Illinois runs a Medicaid programme that pays for care in approved assisted-living-style settings, called the Supportive Living Program. It is not the same as private-pay assisted living and not every community participates. It is explained in detail in Illinois Supportive Living Program, explained.

Medicare does not pay for assisted living. That surprises most families, and the reason is worth understanding before you plan around it — see Does Medicare pay for assisted living or nursing home care?
