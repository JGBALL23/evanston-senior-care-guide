Is it safe to hire a caregiver directly for your parent in Illinois?

Short answer

It can be, but Illinois puts the work on you if you skip an agency. The Illinois Department on Aging draws the line plainly: an agency-employed caregiver has their background check, training and supervision handled by the agency, while a family that hires someone directly is told to “check the IDPH Healthcare Worker Registry before hiring.” That registry records background-check status and any administrative finding of abuse, neglect or misappropriation of property against a direct-care worker — it is free, and it is the one check this guide found Illinois actually telling families to make themselves.

Two ways to bring a caregiver into the house

The Illinois Department on Aging's own page on in-home care splits this into two arrangements, and the difference is who is responsible for vetting the person who will be alone with your parent. For an agency-employed caregiver, the page states plainly: “the agency handles background checks, training, and supervision.” For a directly hired caregiver — someone a family finds and pays without going through an agency — the same page's instruction is just as plain: “check the IDPH Healthcare Worker Registry before hiring.”

Neither arrangement is wrong. But only one of them comes with the check already done for you.

Confirming someone is legally allowed to do this work is a different question from knowing whether paid help at home is the right level of care for your parent in the first place.

What a licensed Illinois home care agency actually is

The Department on Aging's page describes three kinds of agency the Illinois Department of Public Health (IDPH) licenses, and they are not interchangeable. A Home Services Agency provides non-medical personal care and is paid for out of pocket, for a client of any age. A Home Nursing Agency provides skilled nursing as prescribed. A Home Health Agency provides skilled nursing plus physical, occupational and speech therapy, and Medicare, Medicaid or private insurance may cover it. IDPH's own licensing FAQ for Home Services Agencies confirms the licence sits under “Home Health, Home Services, and Home Nursing Agency Code, 77 Ill. Adm. Code 245.”

This is a separate question from the Community Care Program, which pays an agency to deliver in-home service to an eligible older adult at no out-of-pocket cost. This guide is about a family arranging and paying for care itself, inside or outside that programme.

If you hire directly, the state puts the check on you

IDPH describes the Health Care Worker Registry as a list of “individuals with a background check conducted pursuant to the Health Care Worker Background Check Act,” 225 ILCS 46. The registry shows background-check status, training information for certified nursing assistants and other health care workers, and — in IDPH's own words — “displays administrative findings of abuse, neglect or misappropriations of property.”

The Act reaches the specific roles a family is most likely to be hiring for directly: IDPH names “home health care aides, nurse aides, personal care assistants, private duty nurse aides,” alongside similar direct-care roles and employees of licensed long-term care facilities who have contact with residents. Someone with a disqualifying conviction “is prohibited from working in any of the above positions unless a waiver has been granted” by IDPH, and the department states that an employer “must verify registry status of an individual applying for the above positions” before employment. The Department on Aging's own page extends that same check to a family hiring directly, not only to a formal employer.

IDPH names the registry's public search as the way to do this, reachable through the “Search the Health Care Worker Registry” link on its own registry page.

What this guide could not verify, and will not guess at

The IDPH page this guide read describes what the registry contains, but it does not define what a “work eligible” or “not work eligible” result actually means, and it does not walk through what information the search tool asks for. This guide did not open the search tool itself, so it will not describe its screens or guess at how to read a result. Read whatever the tool shows you literally, and call IDPH if a result is unclear rather than assuming what it means.

This guide also could not find a current public list of licensed Illinois home care agencies to check a company itself against, separate from checking an individual worker. Ask an agency for its licence information directly, or confirm it with IDPH, rather than assuming a name or a listing found elsewhere is current.

If something goes wrong after you have hired someone

A concern about a licensed Home Services, Home Nursing or Home Health agency goes to IDPH. A concern about abuse, neglect or exploitation of a parent living at home — whether the caregiver came from an agency or was hired directly — is a different channel, covered in full in What to do about suspected elder abuse or neglect at home in Illinois. This guide is about the check you can make before any of that becomes necessary, not about what to do once it already has.
