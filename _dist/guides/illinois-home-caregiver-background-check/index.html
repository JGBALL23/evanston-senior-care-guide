Is it safe to hire a caregiver directly for your parent in Illinois?

Short answer

It can be, but if you skip an agency, Illinois puts the check on you. The Illinois Department on Aging says that for an agency-employed caregiver, “the agency is responsible for background checks, training, and supervision of the caregiver.” For a caregiver you hire directly, it says “you should check the IDPH Healthcare Worker Registry to ensure the caregiver is eligible to work in-home care.” That registry, kept by the Illinois Department of Public Health, lists people who have had a background check under the Health Care Worker Background Check Act and displays administrative findings of abuse, neglect or misappropriation of property.

Two ways to bring a caregiver into the house

The Illinois Department on Aging’s page on in-home care says “some caregivers are employed by an agency and assigned to you and others are hired directly by you,” and the difference is who vets the person who will be alone with your parent. For an agency-employed caregiver: “the agency is responsible for background checks, training, and supervision of the caregiver.” For a caregiver a family hires directly, the page says “you should check the IDPH Healthcare Worker Registry to ensure the caregiver is eligible to work in-home care.”

Neither arrangement is wrong. Only one of them comes with the check already done for you.

Checking that a worker is eligible for this work is a different question from knowing whether paid help at home is the right level of care for your parent at all.

Three kinds of licensed home care agency

The same page describes three kinds of agency licensed by the Illinois Department of Public Health (IDPH), and they are not interchangeable. A Home Services Agency employs home care aides for personal care and help with daily living, for clients of any age, and the page says “Clients served by Home Services Agencies pay out-of-pocket for care.” A Home Nursing Agency provides skilled nursing “as prescribed by an individual’s healthcare professional.” A Home Health Agency provides “skilled nursing, physical, occupational and speech therapy in the home,” and depending on the circumstances, Medicare, Medicaid or private insurance may cover those services.

IDPH’s own Home Services Agencies page adds: “Home services are not medical.” Its licensing FAQ tells applicants to review the “Home Health, Home Services, and Home Nursing Agency Code, 77 Ill. Adm. Code 245.”

This is a separate question from the Community Care Program, through which Illinois pays for in-home services for some older adults who qualify. The Department on Aging’s page says a family member or friend can work as a caregiver under that program only if they meet its qualifications, pass a background check, complete 24 hours of pre-service training, and are hired and supervised by an in-home services agency.

If you hire directly, the check is yours to make

IDPH says the Health Care Worker Registry “lists individuals with a background check conducted pursuant to the Health Care Worker Background Check Act.” It also shows training information for certified nursing assistants and other health care workers, and IDPH says it “displays administrative findings of abuse, neglect or misappropriations of property.”

The Act covers the roles a family is most likely to hire for. IDPH says it applies to unlicensed people employed or retained by a health care employer as “home health care aides, nurse aides, personal care assistants, private duty nurse aides,” among other direct-care roles. People with disqualifying convictions “are prohibited from working in any of the above positions unless a waiver has been granted” by IDPH, and “A health care employer must verify registry status of an individual applying for the above positions prior to employment.” The Department on Aging’s page tells a family hiring directly to make the same registry check.

IDPH says the check can be made by contacting the department or through its website, where the link reads “Search the Health Care Worker Registry.”

What this guide could not verify

IDPH’s registry page describes what the registry contains but does not explain how to read a search result, and this guide did not use the search tool itself, so it does not describe its screens. Read what the tool shows literally, and contact IDPH if a result is unclear.

To check an agency rather than an individual worker, IDPH’s Home Services Agencies page links to a “Home Services Directory” on the state’s open data portal. This guide has not checked what that directory contains or how current it is, so also ask the agency for its licence details.

If something goes wrong after you hire someone

IDPH’s complaint page lists the Central Complaint Registry Hotline, 800-252-4343, Monday to Friday from 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., and names home health agencies among the providers it covers. IDPH’s Home Services Agencies page also points to that complaint section.

Suspected abuse, neglect or exploitation of a parent who lives at home is a different channel, covered in What to do about suspected elder abuse or neglect at home in Illinois.
