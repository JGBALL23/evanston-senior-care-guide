What Medicare may pay

Short-Term Rehabilitation Costs

No statewide price is shown, because what a family pays depends on insurance, Medicare eligibility, length of stay and the services delivered. Long-stay nursing home medians do not describe a short rehabilitation stay and are not used here as a substitute.

Under Original Medicare, a qualifying stay may carry $0 daily coinsurance for days 1–20, then $217 a day for days 21–100 in 2026, after the applicable Part A deductible. Eligibility rules apply, and Medicare Advantage plans may differ. Confirm with your plan.

Statewide estimates are provided for general comparison and do not represent Westminster Place’s actual rates.

Can Medicaid pay for assisted living? Only in one kind of community

Here is the part that catches families. Traditional assisted living and memory care are usually private-pay. But Illinois runs a separate option: eligible residents can have their care services covered by Medicaid in a state-certified Supportive Living Program setting — including certified dementia-care settings.

Expect two charges, not one. The first is room and board, which stays the resident’s responsibility and is set by the state from the annual Social Security increase: $874 a month for a single-occupancy apartment and $625.50 for double occupancy, effective 1 January 2026 (HFS Provider Notice, 26 November 2025). The second is a group care credit, also called patient liability — an amount calculated from the resident’s countable income after allowable deductions, paid monthly toward the cost of care, with Medicaid covering the remainder. Most residents owe both. A resident avoids the group care credit only when their income equals the SSI amount, and keeps a $120 personal needs allowance either way (HFS Supportive Living resident fact sheet). So $874 is not the cost of living there — it is one line of it. It is a different kind of community with its own eligibility rules, so ask about it by name.

What the words mean

Five kinds of senior living, in plain English

They are not the same thing. Tap one to read more.

Independent Living

An apartment with meals and chores handled. No personal care.

More

A private apartment or cottage, with neighbours on the other side of the door. Meals, housekeeping and transport may be included — services vary by community. Generally no hands-on personal care and no nurse on the floor. Families often skip past it and pay for care nobody needed yet. What independent living actually is

Assisted Living

An apartment with help for daily things.

More

Typically hands-on help with bathing, dressing and medications, in their own apartment. Services vary by community.

Memory Care

A secure home for people with dementia.

More

Usually a secured setting with dementia-trained staff. The question is safety, not just help.

Skilled Nursing

A nursing home. Nurses on duty day and night.

More

Licensed nursing available around the clock. A medical setting, usually a room rather than an apartment.

Short-Term Rehab

A few weeks of therapy after a hospital stay.

More

Weeks, not years, with going home as the goal. Medicare may cover a qualifying stay.

Medicaid, Medicare, Supportive Living and other money words
  • Medicaid — Illinois’s health program for people with low income and savings. It can pay for a nursing home, under its own rules.
  • Medicare — the health insurance most people have from 65. It may pay for a short rehab stay, not for years of care.
  • Supportive Living — Illinois’s Medicaid version of assisted living, in certified communities only.
  • Aid and Attendance — a higher VA pension rate for some veterans and surviving spouses who need daily help.
  • Long-term-care insurance — a policy some people bought years ago. If there is one, it may pay for some care.
  • Private pay — paying from income and savings. How assisted living and memory care in Illinois are usually paid for.

When you visit a place

Twelve questions to ask on a tour

Every place shows you the dining room. These show you the rest.

Show the twelve questions
  1. What is included in the base rate, and what is billed on top?
  2. How do you assess care levels, and how often does the price change as a result?
  3. What is the staff-to-resident ratio overnight?
  4. What is your care staff turnover in the last twelve months?
  5. What happens when my parent's needs exceed what you provide here?
  6. What happens if private funds run out?
  7. How are medications managed, and by whom — a nurse or care staff?
  8. How long has the current executive director been here?
  9. What was your last state survey, and may I see it?
  10. What happens at 2am if my parent falls?
  11. How much notice before a rate increase, and what was last year's?
  12. May I come back unannounced at a mealtime?

One more, and it is the most useful: visit late afternoon. Staffing thins, dementia symptoms often peak, and you see the community as it really runs rather than as it is presented at eleven in the morning.

Who made this, and why there's nothing for sale

I work at Westminster Place in Evanston. This guide is my own project, and nobody pays for a result.

More about how this guide works

I kept watching families arrive at a decision this large already exhausted, holding four words nobody had ever explained to them. The explanation is not complicated. It just was not written down anywhere in plain language, for this town.

The assessment cannot be steered. It reads your six answers against published rules and returns a level of care — never a community. It asks nothing about who you are and it takes no fees from anyone. I work at Westminster Place in Evanston. After the result I show you their information, clearly labelled, alongside the state’s own licensed-facility directory so you can see every other option. I am not going to pretend to rank communities when I work at one of them.

This is a general guide, not medical advice and not a clinical assessment. Only a doctor, nurse or licensed care assessor can determine what level of care someone needs. Use this to understand your options and ask sharper questions — then confirm with a professional.

Sources & methodology where these numbers come from, and how the assessment decides

How the assessment works

It is an educational starting point, not a medical diagnosis. Your six answers are matched against a short list of published decision rules, in a fixed order, and the first rule that fits decides the category. Those rules live in assessment.js on this site — you can open it and read them. The result always quotes the answers that produced it, so the reasoning can be checked rather than trusted.

What this site does not do

  • No community is ranked, scored or compared. The assessment returns a level of care and nothing else.
  • Nobody pays to influence a result. There is no advertising, no sponsorship, and no referral fee from any community — including Westminster Place, where I work.
  • Your assessment answers are never collected, stored or transmitted. The assessment runs entirely in your browser and no health information leaves this device.
  • There is exactly one place that asks you for anything: the optional tour-inquiry form shown after the result. It asks for a name, a phone number or an email address, and which type of care you are interested in, and nothing else. If you tick the permission box, those details, with your care level if you took the assessment, are passed by hand to Westminster Place Admissions so they can contact you about a tour, and a dated record is kept here showing the inquiry started on this site. It is kept completely separate from the assessment. Skipping it changes nothing about your result, and Westminster’s own form and phone number are on the page either way.
  • Apart from that form, the site counts how many people reach each step — arriving, starting the questions, seeing a result, opening the form — and which care category a result was. It is a count, not a record of you: no cookie, no identifier, and no answers. The privacy notice explains exactly how.
  • Costs shown are published estimates and medians. Actual prices vary by community, apartment, care level and year.
  • Coverage and eligibility rules change, and they depend on your parent's specific circumstances. Confirm anything that matters with the agency or plan directly.
