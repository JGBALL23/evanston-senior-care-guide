## Required corrections

**Correction 1 (Q21, scope).** In "If you think the discharge is too soon", replace "Under Original Medicare, the page says that if you ask in time &ldquo;you can stay in the hospital ... deductibles).&rdquo;" with:

> In a hospital, the page says that if your parent asks in time &ldquo;you can stay in the hospital while you wait to get the BFCC-QIO&rsquo;s decision. You won&rsquo;t have to pay for your stay (except for applicable coinsurance or deductibles).&rdquo; The page does not limit this to Original Medicare: it says that in a hospital, &ldquo;If you have a Medicare health plan, the BFCC-QIO will notify your plan and the hospital.&rdquo;

Source sentences (S4, "How do I ask for a fast appeal?" — "In a hospital:"): "If you ask for your appeal within this time frame, you can stay in the hospital while you wait to get the BFCC-QIO's decision. You won't have to pay for your stay (except for applicable coinsurance or deductibles)." and (S4, "What will happen during the BFCC-QIO's review?" — "In a hospital:"): "The BFCC-QIO will notify the hospital. If you have a Medicare health plan, the BFCC-QIO will notify your plan and the hospital."

**Correction 2 (Q23, scope).** Replace "For a parent in a Medicare Advantage plan, the page says: &ldquo;you can ask your plan for an appeal, but different rules apply.&rdquo;" with:

> For a parent in a Medicare Advantage plan, the page says that if the BFCC-QIO decides the stay should continue, Medicare keeps covering it &ldquo;if your plan previously authorized coverage of the inpatient admission, or the inpatient admission was for emergency or urgently needed care.&rdquo; Right after the rule for a missed deadline, it adds: &ldquo;If you&rsquo;re in a Medicare Advantage Plan, you can ask your plan for an appeal, but different rules apply.&rdquo;

Source sentences (S4, "What does the BFCC-QIO's decision mean for my appeal?" — "In a hospital:"): "And you have a Medicare health plan: Medicare will continue to cover your hospital stay as long as medically necessary (except for applicable coinsurance or deductibles) if your plan previously authorized coverage of the inpatient admission, or the inpatient admission was for emergency or urgently needed care." and (S4, "How do I ask for a fast appeal?" — "In a hospital:", following the missed-deadline sentence): "If you're in a Medicare Advantage Plan, you can ask your plan for an appeal, but different rules apply."

## Recommended additions (Improve; not a bar to publish-ready; verify in a later session)

- **R1 Missed deadline (all Medicare, in a hospital).** S4 "How do I ask for a fast appeal?": "If you miss the deadline for a fast appeal, you can still ask the BFCC-QIO to review your case, but different rules and time frames apply". The guide gives only the cost warning, not that review is still possible.
- **R2 What it costs if the appeal is lost (all Medicare, in a hospital, deadline met).** S4 "What does the BFCC-QIO's decision mean for my appeal?" — "In a hospital:": "If they decide your services should end: If you met the deadline for asking for a fast appeal, you won't be responsible for paying the hospital charges (except for applicable coinsurance or deductibles) incurred through noon of the day after the BFCC-QIO gives you its decision." and "If you get any inpatient hospital services after noon of that day, you may have to pay for them."
- **R3 Rehab costs (Part A, 2026; MA different).** S3 "Costs": "Days 1–20: You pay $0 each day after you pay the $1,736 deductible ($1,776 in 2027)." "Days 21–100: You pay $217 each day ($222 in 2027)." "Days 101 and beyond: You pay all costs." "If you're in a Medicare Advantage Plan, you may be charged copayments during the first 20 days." The guide's SNF section gives no cost.
- **R4 Inpatient changed to observation (any Medicare, in a hospital).** S4 intro: "You can ask for a fast appeal while you're still in the hospital if you were admitted to the hospital as an inpatient and the hospital changed your status to “outpatient getting observation services.”" S3 "Coverage details": "You can appeal past hospital stays back to January 2009". Directly bears on the guide's 3-day-rule section.
- **R5 Remaining SNF eligibility conditions (Part A).** S3 "Who's eligible": "You have Part A and have days left in your benefit period to use." and skilled services needed for "An ongoing condition that was also treated during your qualifying inpatient hospital stay (even if it wasn't the reason you were admitted to the hospital)" or a new condition that started during SNF care for it.
- **R6 Free help with appeals (all Medicare, national).** S1 resources: "State Health Insurance Assistance Programs (SHIPs) Offer free counseling on health insurance and programs for people with limited income. Also help with claims, billing, and appeals. Visit shiphelp.org, or call 1-800-MEDICARE to get your SHIP's phone number."
- **R7 Hospital's own SNF (Part A SNF care).** S3 "Facility": "If the hospital you're in has its own SNF, and a bed is available, you may choose to stay there."
