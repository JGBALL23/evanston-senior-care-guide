## Recommended additions (not defects; apply as Improve, to be verified by a later session)

1. **Which Illinois Medicaid group covers an older parent.** Source: HFS, Introduction to Medicaid, heading "Aid to the Aged Blind or Disabled (AABD)": "Aid to the Aged Blind or Disabled (AABD) provides health coverage to people who are age 65 or older, or are blind, or have a disability." Scope: Illinois, people 65+ or blind or disabled. On the same page, the screening tool asks for "basic information about your household and income" through www.Getcoveredillinois.gov or 1-866-311-1119 (heading "Eligibility Screening Tool"). Suggested addition: name AABD as the group an older parent applies under, and say that the screening asks only about household and income, so it does not test assets.
2. **A parent in Medicare Advantage who moves out of the plan's area and goes back to Original Medicare has a federal Medigap guaranteed issue right.** Source: Medicare.gov publication 02110, "Choosing a Medigap Policy" (PDF modified 5 March 2026), https://www.medicare.gov/publications/02110-medigap-guide-health-insurance.pdf, Section 3 table: "You have a Medicare Advantage Plan, and any of the following are true: ... You move out of the plan's service area" -> "Medigap Plan A, B, C*, D*, F*, or G* that's sold by an insurance company in your state", applying "60 days before the date your Medicare Advantage Plan coverage ends (if you are choosing to leave the plan)" or "No more than 63 days after your Medicare Advantage Plan coverage ends", and "You only have this right if you switch to Original Medicare (rather than join another Medicare Advantage Plan)." Scope: federal, MA enrollees only. This belongs beside Q10, because a parent dropped to Original Medicare would want a Medigap policy without medical questions.
3. **Illinois's residence rule.** Source: 89 Ill. Adm. Code 120.311, "Residence" (read via Cornell LII; cite the ilga.gov copy, https://ilga.gov/ftp/JCAR/AdminCode/089/089001200H03110R.html, after a later session reads it there, since ilga.gov did not answer from this session). It defines residence as living in Illinois, sets no minimum length of time, and lists exceptions, including a person who maintains a home in another state and "the individual was placed in an institution located in Illinois by another State." Scope: Illinois medical assistance, all settings. It would let the guide answer "how long" for settings other than a nursing home, rather than only saying that no HFS page sets a minimum.
4. **By when to apply for nursing home Medicaid.** Source: HFS ltc_faq.pdf (6 November 2014), Q3.2: "Medicaid coverage may be backdated for up to 3 months prior to the application month." Scope: Illinois Medicaid applications for long-term care, in a sheet dated 2014. The guide must give the date and say the sheet was written for facilities.
