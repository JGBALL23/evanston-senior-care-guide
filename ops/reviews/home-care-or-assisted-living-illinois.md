## Recommended additions

1. More hours for a short time (Medicare home health, Original Medicare scope; for the "How many hours" decision). Medicare.gov Home health services, "How often": "You may be able to get more frequent care for a short time (less than 8 hours a day and up to 35 hours a week) if your provider decides it's necessary."
2. Illinois pre-claim review (Medicare home health from an Illinois agency; every reader of this guide). Medicare.gov Home health services, "Coverage details", help drawer "Do you get services from a home health agency in Florida, Illinois, Ohio, North Carolina, or Texas?": "you may be affected by a Medicare demonstration program. Under this demonstration, your home health agency may submit a request for pre-claim review of coverage for home health services to Medicare. This helps you and the home health agency know earlier in the process if Medicare is likely to cover the services."
3. A spouse can be the paid caregiver (CCP; the spouse at home). IDoA In-Home Care, "Hiring a Family Member or Friend to be your Caregiver": "Any family member (including a spouse), a Power of Attorney, representative payee, legal guardian, friend or neighbor must meet qualifications, pass a background check, complete 24-hours of pre-service training, and be hired and supervised by an In-Home Services agency before serving as a paid caregiver."
4. Whom to call (Illinois). The guide names Care Coordination Units and "the Department's map tool" without a link or number. IDoA CCP, "How Do I Access Services?": "To find your local CCU, use our map tool." Page header: "Senior HelpLine: 1-800-252-8966 (711 TRS)". Link the map tool and give the HelpLine.
5. Supportive Living money rules (Medicaid-eligible SLP residents; private pay residents). HFS Resident Fact Sheet, "Rates:": "Each Medicaid-eligible resident must have income equal to or greater than the current SSI and must contribute all but $120 (PNA) each month to the provider for lodging and meals." and "Certified providers can charge a different rate for private pay residents". "Resident's Cost of Care Liability:": "Additionally, all excess resources will be counted towards the cost of care."
6. Other payers for long-term care (all Medicare beneficiaries). Medicare.gov Long-term care, "Who's eligible": "Although you're not eligible for long-term care under Medicare, you may be eligible for it through Medicaid (if you meet eligibility requirements in your state), or you can choose to buy private long-term care insurance."
