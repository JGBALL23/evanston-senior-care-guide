- Answer box: "a problem with the care inside a nursing home or assisted living facility goes to the Long-Term Care Ombudsman or the Illinois Department of Public Health instead." Source: brochure, "What is the Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program?": "The Ombudsman Program is available to assist with alleged abuse, neglect and financial exploitation by an abuser living in or working for the facility" (long-term care facilities include "a nursing home, assisted living"); "What if abuse, neglect or exploitation is happening to a resident of a long-term care facility?": ombudsmen refer to law enforcement or "the Illinois Department of Public Health, which regulates most long-term care facilities." Same scope.
- Answer box: "The exception ...: if a facility resident is abused outside the facility, or financially exploited, by a family member, caregiver or another person with a relationship to them, "the APS Program may respond."" Source: brochure, "What about adults who reside in a facility but are abused or exploited from someone they know outside the facility?": "if an individual is allegedly abused outside of a facility by a family member, caregiver or another person with a relationship to them, the APS Program may respond. Further, if a resident of a long-term care facility is allegedly financially exploited by a family member, caregiver or another person with a relationship with them, the APS Program may respond." Same scope. (Recommended addition, not a bar: the next sentence, same heading, scope a facility resident in that situation: "The resident may choose to work with the APS Program and/or the Ombudsman Program in this situation.")
- First section: "A concern about the care a nursing home or assisted living community gives is a different problem with a different number — the Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program or the Illinois Department of Public Health's Central Complaint Registry". Source: as the first line above. Same scope. (Note, unchanged sentence: the section still opens "Illinois splits elder-safety reporting ... by where the person lives" with APS for anyone "outside a licensed care facility"; the exception appears only in the answer box. Not reopened on this, since the changed sentence is right, but the next edit of this section should carry the exception into the body.)
- Rights: "The Engage page also lists the older adult's own rights, among them to "Choose where and how they will live" and to "Choose whether to accept social services or other community assistance", so a report starts a conversation with your parent, not a decision made for them." Source: Engage, "What Happens When a Report Is Made?" / "1. Your Rights": the same list continues "Make decisions different from those that we might make, including "bad" decisions, as long as those decisions don't hurt themselves or others" and "When an adult with a disability or older adult is incapable of protecting himself or herself, under the law that person has the right to have protective measures taken on his or her behalf". SCOPE DEFECT: the quoted rights hold for an adult who can protect themselves and whose choices do not hurt themselves or others; the guide's conclusion "not a decision made for them" is given for every parent, directly after the self-neglect example (a parent not eating or taking medication), which is the case the source limits. Corrected sentence: "The Engage page also lists the older adult's own rights, among them to "Choose where and how they will live" and to "Choose whether to accept social services or other community assistance", as long as those decisions "don't hurt themselves or others"; when an older adult "is incapable of protecting himself or herself," the same list says that person "has the right to have protective measures taken on his or her behalf.""
- Mandated reporters: "the Engage page says Illinois law requires certain professionals to report suspected abuse of adults age 60 or older, or people with disabilities age 18-59 who are unable, due to dysfunction, to report for themselves: people delivering professional services to them in social services, adult care, law enforcement, education, medicine, state service to seniors and social work." Source: Engage, "Who are Adult Protective Service mandated reporters?": "Illinois has a law which requires certain professionals to make reports of suspected abuse of adults age 60 or older or people with disabilities age 18-59 who are unable, due to dysfunction, to report for themselves." (no comma after "60 or older"); IDoA Report Abuse page, "Professionals Required to Report": "*Mandatory reporting requirements only apply when the reporter believes that the adult is not capable of reporting the abuse, neglect, or financial exploitation themselves". SCOPE DEFECT: the comma the guide inserted limits the "unable, due to dysfunction" condition to people 18-59, so the guide states a duty to report abuse of every adult 60 or older; the department says the duty applies only when the adult is not capable of reporting it themselves, at any age. Corrected sentence: "On who must report, the Engage page says Illinois law requires certain professionals to report suspected abuse of adults age 60 or older or people with disabilities age 18-59 who are unable, due to dysfunction, to report for themselves: people delivering professional services to them in social services, adult care, law enforcement, education, medicine, state service to seniors and social work; the department's Report Abuse page adds that "Mandatory reporting requirements only apply when the reporter believes that the adult is not capable of reporting the abuse, neglect, or financial exploitation themselves"." Add https://ilaging.illinois.gov/protectionadvocacy/abuse-reporting.html to the sources.
- "A family member does not have to be sure: the same page says "Report it, even if you are not sure."" Source: Engage, "What if I am not sure if I should make a report?": "Report it, even if you are not sure." Same scope (addressed to anyone reporting).
- Sources list: new entry "Illinois Department on Aging — Adult Protective Services and Ombudsman Program (#Engage2Change brochure, printed 1/21)" with the PDF URL. Same; link live.

Reopened: two scope defects in changed sentences: (1) the adult's-rights sentence drops the source's limits ("as long as those decisions don't hurt themselves or others"; protective measures for an adult "incapable of protecting himself or herself") and concludes "not a decision made for them" for every parent; (2) the mandated-reporter sentence inserts a comma that makes the duty cover all adults 60+, while IDoA says it applies only when the reporter believes the adult is not capable of reporting it themselves. Corrected sentences above.
